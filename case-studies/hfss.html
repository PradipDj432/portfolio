Case studyHFSS

A custodial platform for EUR and crypto.

HFSS (Helvetia Financial Services) is a Swiss fintech platform. Clients open EUR accounts, hold crypto, send and receive payments and trade, while staff and an OTC desk run it from their own tools. Built on Node.js, Fastify, Next.js, PostgreSQL and AWS.

01Problem

The company holds the money, so every number must be exact.

HFSS keeps EUR and crypto for its clients, who are people and companies. Every client must pass identity checks first (KYC for people, KYB for companies). Clients want a EUR account with its own IBAN, SEPA payments, a crypto wallet and trading. Staff need tools to approve payouts, review clients, set fees and limits, and freeze accounts. An OTC desk needs its own screens for big trades.

The hard part: the outside providers keep all clients' money in shared pools, so they only see one big balance. The platform is the only place that knows how much belongs to each client.

02Solution

Three apps and one money engine.

  • Client app: sign-up and login with 2-step verification, identity checks with Sumsub, a EUR account, saved payees, sending and receiving money, a crypto wallet, trading and conversion, in four languages.
  • Backoffice for staff: approving or rejecting payouts, client and compliance review, fee and limit plans, subscriptions and invoices, audit logs, and freeze controls for a whole account, withdrawals only or trading only.
  • OTC desk app: a separate app where operators handle brokers, EUR and crypto trades, custody and the ledger.
  • Provider integrations: Delos, Lorum and Fiat Republic for EUR accounts and payments, FinchTrade and Kraken for exchange, Fireblocks for crypto custody, and Sumsub for identity checks.
  • Money engine: a double-entry ledger, fee and limit rules, monthly billing and daily background checks.

03Architecture

Next.js apps in front, a Fastify API and PostgreSQL behind.

  1. Front end: Next.js and React apps for clients, staff and the OTC desk, in TypeScript.
  2. Back end: a Node.js and Fastify API in TypeScript, with background jobs for daily checks and retries.
  3. Data: PostgreSQL on AWS RDS, with numbered SQL migrations.
  4. Providers: signed webhooks come in and are checked before use; calls out to providers carry their own repeat keys.
  5. Infrastructure: AWS ECS, with all infrastructure written in Terraform.
  6. Delivery: GitHub Actions builds, runs database migrations and deploys.

05Challenges

Exact balances, no double payments.

  • Exact balances in a shared pool. Every money move is booked on two sides in a double-entry ledger, and every day the platform's client balances are checked against each provider's real balance.
  • No double payments. A money step runs in one database transaction, balances are locked while they change, and every request carries a repeat key, so a double click or retry pays only once.
  • Unclear answers. When a provider gives an unclear answer, the money is held for checking and never refunded blindly.
  • Trusting nothing from outside. Every webhook is signature-checked before it is used.
  • Many providers. Six outside providers, each with different rules, behind one consistent set of screens.

06Results

Live, with real clients and real money.

The platform is live and serves 100+ clients, with EUR 10M+ in transaction value each month. I wrote 70%+ of the backend commits, and the money rules are covered by 1,700+ automated tests.
